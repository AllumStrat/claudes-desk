# Anti-AI Writing

Rules for prose that doesn't read as AI-generated. Apply all of them to any prose you write or edit. Rules only, no justification unless it disambiguates.

## Banned vocabulary — never use these

**Puffery / significance inflation:**
delve, tapestry, vibrant, rich (figurative), profound, groundbreaking (figurative), renowned, nestled, in the heart of, boasts, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty

**Abstract filler:**
crucial, pivotal, vital, key (adjective), valuable, intricate/intricacies, interplay, enduring, garner, align with, resonate with, encompasses, cultivating, fostering (figurative)

**Weak verb substitutes:**
underscore (as verb), highlight (as verb), emphasize (as verb), reflect (figurative), symbolize, contribute to, enhance

**Transition clichés:**
Additionally (sentence-opener), Furthermore, Moreover, It is worth noting, It is important to note

**Sycophantic openers:**
Certainly!, Of course!, Great question!, Absolutely!, I hope this helps, Let me know if you need anything else

**Legacy / impact phrases:**
stands as a testament, marks a pivotal moment, setting the stage for, evolving landscape, focal point, indelible mark, deeply rooted, lasting legacy, broader trends, represents a shift

## Core rules

1. **Use "is/are" over elaborate substitutes.** BAD: "serves as," "stands as," "marks," "represents," "features," "boasts". GOOD: "is," "has," "was".
2. **Never inflate significance.** If a fact is mundane, say it plainly. Do not explain why it matters unless the source says so.
3. **No vague attribution.** BAD: "Experts argue," "Observers note," "Several sources suggest," "Critics have described". GOOD: name the source, or drop the claim.
4. **No superficial analysis appended via -ing phrases.** BAD: "The report was released, highlighting its importance for regional development." GOOD: "The report was released." (If the importance is sourced, state it as a separate sentence with attribution.)
5. **No rule-of-three stacking unless the list is genuinely exhaustive and meaningful.** BAD: "dynamic, innovative, and forward-thinking approach". GOOD: pick one specific word or none.
6. **No "not only X but Y" parallelisms.** BAD: "Not just a building, it's a symbol of community." GOOD: "It is a community center."
7. **No false ranges.** BAD: "from technology to tradition," "from science to storytelling". GOOD: list items directly, or omit.
8. **No elegant variation to dodge repetition.** Repeat the noun. Do not substitute: protagonist, key player, eponymous figure, the aforementioned, said [noun].
9. **No "Despite its [positive], [subject] faces challenges" formula.**
10. **No title case in section headings.** Sentence case only.
11. **No emoji in professional writing.**
12. **Never use the em dash (—).** Use a comma, parenthesis, colon, or period instead.
13. **No bullet + bold-header lists** (e.g., **Term**: description) unless a glossary is explicitly requested.
14. **No promotional language about companies, places, or people** not sourced to a promotional context.

## If/then decision logic

- **IF** about to write "serves as" or "stands as" → write "is".
- **IF** about to write "Additionally" at the start of a sentence → delete it, or restructure to connect ideas directly.
- **IF** about to explain why something is significant → ask: does the source say this? If no, cut it.
- **IF** a sentence ends in an -ing clause that explains impact → cut the clause, keep the fact.
- **IF** stacking three adjectives → pick one or rewrite as a concrete detail.
- **IF** attributing to "experts," "observers," or "critics" with no name → name the source or remove the claim.
- **IF** describing challenges at the end of a piece → check for the formula [positive] → [faces challenges] → [optimistic close]. If present, break it.
- **IF** the writing sounds like a press release or travel brochure → strip adjectives, flatten claims to facts.
- **IF** more than one banned word appears in a paragraph → rewrite the paragraph.

## Reusable structures

**Introducing a fact (plain):** `[Subject] [verb] [object] in [year/context].`

**Citing an opinion:** `According to [Named Source], [claim].` Not: `Experts argue that [claim].`

**Describing a place or organization:** `[Subject] is a [type] in [location]. It [does/has] [specific thing].` Not: `Nestled in the heart of [location], [Subject] boasts a vibrant community and rich cultural heritage.`

**Describing impact (only if sourced):** `[Source] described it as [quoted claim].` Not: `This highlights its enduring significance for [broader trend].`

**Transitioning between ideas:** Use the last word/idea of the previous sentence to open the next. No transition adverb needed.

## Common failure patterns

| Pattern | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Significance inflation | "marking a pivotal moment in..." | Delete the clause |
| Vague attribution | "Critics have noted..." | Name the critic or cut |
| -ing analysis append | "...reflecting its broader impact." | Cut after the period |
| Rule of three | "innovative, dynamic, and forward-thinking" | One concrete word |
| Elegant variation | "the CEO... the executive... the leader" | Repeat "the CEO" |
| Promotional language | "nestled in the heart of," "boasts" | Rewrite with "is/has" |
| AI opener | "Certainly! Great question." | Never use. Start with the answer. |
| Challenge formula | "Despite its success, X faces challenges..." | Write challenges as plain facts |
| False range | "from ancient history to modern innovation" | List items directly |

## Good vs bad

**BAD:** Nestled in the heart of Barcelona, the institute stands as a vibrant testament to innovation, fostering collaboration across disciplines and contributing to the broader landscape of European research.
**GOOD:** The institute is in Barcelona. It runs joint programs across biology, chemistry, and computer science departments.

**BAD:** Additionally, the study highlights the crucial role of community engagement, underscoring the enduring importance of local participation in shaping policy outcomes.
**GOOD:** The study found that communities with active local councils adopted the policy 40% faster.

**BAD:** Experts argue that the film represents a pivotal shift in cinematic storytelling, reflecting broader changes in audience expectations.
**GOOD:** Roger Ebert called it "the first Hollywood film to treat silence as a dramatic tool."

**BAD:** Despite its many achievements, the organization faces several challenges, including funding constraints and staffing shortages. With continued investment, however, it is poised to fulfill its mission.
**GOOD:** The organization has not met its fundraising target in three of the last five years. It currently has four open staff positions.

**BAD (em dash):** The decision was clear — they had to cut the budget.
**GOOD:** The decision was clear: they had to cut the budget.
